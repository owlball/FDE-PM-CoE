    FDE PM Center of Excellence

    CoE Charter v1.4

    One consistent way of working across Discovery and Execution — standards, handoffs, and working agreements for the FDE PM practice.

    11/01/2026 · App AI  |  Sponsor: Nitin Ghagare  |  PM Lead: Camilo Ballesteros

    1. Purpose

    Give FDE product managers one consistent way of working across Discovery and Execution, so quality and handoffs don't depend on who is assigned. The group sets the standards, shares what it learns, and keeps context from getting lost between phases.

    2. Mission

    Provide the standards, templates, and peer support that make FDE PMs effective in both phases, and keep field learning from staying in individual PMs' heads.

    3. Objectives

    1. Define what good looks like for the Discovery and Execution PM roles.
    2. Standardize the artifacts Discovery produces and Execution receives.
    3. Make the PM-to-PM handoff reliable.
    4. Maintain a technical bar for PMs working in Discovery, who build prototypes while stress-testing use cases.
    5. Capture assumptions, stress-test results, and reuse candidates in one shared place.
    6. Make daily use of Claude a consistent, responsible part of how the group works.

    4. Scope

    In scope: PM role standards; Discovery and Execution playbooks; templates, specs, handoff package; the handoff standard; peer review of Discovery packages; PM-level scope management within the 90-working-day Execution window; field insight and reuse capture; working agreements for the group; PM onboarding and development.

    Out of scope: the Go/No-Go decision and panel (already established); the value case and savings forecast (Value Advisor); line management; core product roadmap; pricing and contracts.

    5. Interfaces

    FunctionRelationship
    Go/No-Go panel The group sets the standard for the Discovery package PMs submit; it does not influence decisions
    Value Advisor The Value Advisor owns the value case and works from their own templates. Our work might help inform these documents
    Core Product PMs pass on structured field insights and reuse candidates; Core Product owns the roadmap
    FDE Engineering Shared prototype and handoff expectations

    6. PM Standards

    The Discovery role

    • Produces the product spec and functional spec for the use case, which Execution builds against
    • Produces the as-is, to-be that the specs are grounded in
    • Builds prototypes sufficient to validate feasibility and records each stress-test result
    • Declares a prototype disposition at handoff: reference only, baseline to extend, or discard
    • States the evidence behind each assumption marked validated, invalidated, or open
    • Runs the use case through SAP's AI tools to evaluate feasibility against the client's software version, records the result, and documents and resolves any disagreement with prototype or stress-test findings

    The Execution role

    • Formally accepts or returns the handoff package
    • Owns scope, sequencing, and trade-offs inside the 90-working-day window
    • Escalates scope drift and overrun risk early
    • Captures learnings and reuse candidates during delivery

    Handoff standard (Discovery → Execution)

    • Package: Standard specs, logic diagram, User Journey, UX state diagram, UI SPA, prototype
    • Overlap: the Execution PM joins the last stretch of Discovery; the Discovery PM stays reachable for the first few weeks of Execution
    • Acceptance: written acceptance, or specific gaps returned
    • Peer check: feasibility is first evaluated with SAP's AI tools. Another PM in the group reviews before the panel when (a) the tool's result is inconclusive or the use case falls outside its coverage, (b) the tool and the prototype or stress tests disagree, or (c) the feasibility claim rests only on the author's own prototype without tool confirmation. The reviewing PM remains accountable for the judgment.

    Moving between roles

    • Assignments are stable. A PM moves between Discovery and Execution only when need arises and onboarding a new PM isn't possible, and no more often than once per quarter.
    • Shadowing a Discovery engagement beforehand can be proposed but is not required, given time constraints.
    • The default support is a buddy: a PM with experience in the phase tags along for the first few calls (about 3) of the new PM's first engagement in that phase, then stays available for questions.
    • A PM leaving an engagement mid-stream follows the same handoff standard as a Discovery-to-Execution handoff.

    7. Operating Model

    • Weekly: 30-minute team sync covering active engagements, blockers, upcoming handoffs, and assignments and capacity
    • Per handoff: the peer check and a short handoff conversation
    • Monthly: one working session on a topic (template improvement, a hard stress test, a postmortem, Claude workflows that worked)
    • Quarterly: retrospective on handoffs and insights, template review, and review of whether any role moves are needed, with Sponsor
    • Shared repository: one place for templates, past assumption registers, stress tests, prompts and workflows, and lessons

    8. Ways of Working and Expectations

    These working agreements exist so PMs can be reached, absences don't surprise anyone, and nobody has to guess what is expected. Where ACN or SAP policy already covers an item, that policy governs and this section points to it.

    A. Availability and reachability

    ExpectationDetail
    Work inside the VDIDuring the workday, stay logged into your VDI. Teams messages and urgent requests from managers reach us only there, not on phones or ACN systems.
    Working hoursCore hours may vary by engagement and client location. Should be agreed upon and communicated by engagement.
    Teams statusKeep your status accurate (available, in a meeting, away, out of office)
    Response timesTeams message: within 1 hour during core hours. Urgent request: acknowledge within 15 minutes.
    If your VDI is downTell the PM Lead right away through ACN MS Teams and work on what you can offline. Don't go silent.
    Backup channelMS Teams ACN side.

    B. Working with Claude

    Claude is approved for production work at SAP, and the group expects to use it daily. Uses range from feeding in transcripts for context, to drafting artifacts, to building single-page apps to prototype a UI, to building applications.

    ExpectationDetail
    Daily useUse Claude as a normal part of your workday
    Know the data rulesConfirm with your SAP manager which data classes the approval covers (for example, customer transcripts, personal data, confidential client information) and follow them.
    You own the outputYou are accountable for anything Claude or any other AI produces. Check facts, numbers, and customer-specific claims before they go into a package or reach a panel.
    AI-built prototypes follow the same rulesA recorded disposition is required, and feasibility claims resting on them get the peer check
    Production-bound work gets reviewAnything built with Claude that is headed toward production goes through normal engineering review, not just a PM's own check
    Share what worksAdd useful prompts, workflows, and examples to the repository

    C. Planned and unplanned absences

    ExpectationDetail
    PTO trackerEvery absence goes in the shared tracker, including partial days
    Planned absenceAt least one week's notice to the practice, and update the tracker. Tell your engagement partners yourself.
    Longer or high-risk absencesFor more than 3 days, or during a handoff or panel window, tell the PM Lead as early as you can so coverage can be planned
    CoverageName a backup for each active engagement and write a short handover note before you leave
    Unplanned absenceNotify the PM Lead and your engagement partners as soon as you can, and update the tracker
    HolidaysAccount for holidays in every region the group works across; keep them visible in the tracker
    Time reportingFollow your employer's requirements (ACN or SAP) separately from the tracker

    D. Communication and visibility

    ExpectationDetail
    Weekly syncAttend, series scheduled for 30 minutes
    Engagement statusKeep each engagement's status line current: phase, risks, upcoming handoff or panel dates
    Raise blockers earlyFlag them in the weekly sync or sooner
    Keep work discoverablePut artifacts in the repository, not in personal drives, private chats, or only in your head
    Use the right channelQuick questions in Teams; decisions and rationale written in the engagement record

    E. Reviews and role changes

    ExpectationDetail
    Peer review turnaroundRespond to peer-check requests within 2 business days
    No self-reviewThe person who built a prototype or wrote a feasibility claim doesn't review it
    Role changesPer section 6: rare, no more than quarterly, with a buddy rather than required shadowing
    Stay broadly skilledKeep a working grasp of both phases so a move is possible if one is needed

    F. Confidentiality and conduct

    ExpectationDetail
    Customer and SAP dataHandle it per SAP and ACN policy; keep it inside approved systems and tools
    MeetingsCome prepared and on time; record agreed decisions
    Respect and candorGive feedback directly and constructively; disagree openly, then commit to the decision
    Ask for helpRaise capacity or skill gaps early. It's expected, not a failure.

    9. Governance and Decision Rights

    DecisionOwner
    PM standards, templates, competency barPM Practice
    Exceptions to the handoff standardPM Lead
    Scope and prioritization within an engagementAssigned PM
    A PM moving between rolesSAP/ACN leadership
    Standards that affect the panel's inputsRaised with the panel owner; not decided here
    Priorities, staffing, and direction of the groupSponsor, with PM Lead

    Escalation: PM → PM Lead → Sponsor

    10. Roles

    RoleResponsibilities
    SponsorSponsors the group, removes blockers, reviews quarterly results
    PM LeadOwns standards, templates, cadence, and the repository; coaches PMs; reports to Sponsor
    PMsWork in the Discovery or Execution role as assigned; contribute to PM Practice; act as peer reviewers and buddies

    Rotate ownership of the repository and the monthly session so the load isn't all on the PM Lead.

    11. Success Measures

    With a small team and a handful of engagements, percentages will swing on one data point. Track counts and pair them with qualitative review in the quarterly retro.

    AreaMeasure
    HandoffsPackages accepted first time (count); issues found in Execution traced to handoff gaps
    Discovery rigorAssumptions that proved wrong in Execution but weren't flagged in Discovery
    PrototypesDisposition recorded on every handoff; baseline prototypes that needed major rework
    ExecutionEngagements delivered within 90 working days; number of scope changes
    ReuseReuse candidates logged, and how many Core Product or another team picked up
    TeamQuarterly PM feedback on the usefulness of templates and standards; panel feedback on package quality; Claude workflows added to the repository

    12. Roadmap

    • First 30 days: Talk with each PM and map how handoffs and Discovery artifacts work today; agree first versions of the handoff template and prototype disposition; stand up the PTO tracker and agree the ways-of-working items
    • Days 30–90: Start the weekly sync and peer check; set up the repository; run the first handoff under the new standard
    • 3–6 months: Refine templates from real handoffs; first quarterly retro with Sponsor; define baseline measures
    • 6–12 months: Role standards and onboarding guide for new PMs; first view of reuse outcomes

    13. Risks

    RiskMitigation
    Context loss at handoffOverlap, written acceptance, standard package
    A PM anchors on their own prototypePeer check before the panel
    Prototype becomes production by accidentDisposition required; Execution PM decides rebuild versus extend
    A PM is moved to an unfamiliar phaseBuddy on first calls; shadowing offered when time allows; peer support
    Capacity gaps with no forward visibilityAssignments and capacity reviewed weekly; named backup on every engagement
    Claude output goes uncheckedOwner accountability; peer check; engineering review for production-bound work
    Reachability gaps for ACN colleaguesVDI expectation; backup channel; escalation to PM Lead
    Process overhead slows a small teamKeep templates short; review quarterly and drop what isn't used
    PM Lead becomes a bottleneckRotate repository and session ownership; peer reviewers share the load
    Seen as overreaching into panel or value workExplicit scope boundary and interface table
